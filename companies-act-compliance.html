Overview

Comprehensive Companies Act Compliance

The Companies Act 2013 is the primary legislation governing the incorporation, management, and dissolution of companies in India. It replaced the Companies Act 1956 and introduced significant changes to corporate governance, director responsibilities, meeting requirements, and disclosure obligations. The Act, along with its rules and amendments, imposes a wide range of compliance obligations on companies of all types and sizes.

Navigating the complexities of the Companies Act requires specialized knowledge and consistent attention. The Act contains 470 sections and numerous schedules, with compliance requirements ranging from board meeting procedures and director appointment rules to related party transaction disclosures and statutory register maintenance. Non-compliance can attract severe penalties — including fines of up to ₹25 lakhs for the company and ₹5 lakhs for officers in default — and in certain cases, imprisonment.

Board Meetings

Board Meeting Requirements

Section 173 of the Companies Act 2013 prescribes specific requirements for convening and conducting board meetings.

Meeting Frequency & Notice

  • Minimum 4 board meetings per year
  • Maximum gap of 120 days between two consecutive meetings
  • First board meeting within 30 days of incorporation
  • Notice of at least 7 days before the meeting
  • Shorter notice allowed with consent of all directors

Quorum & Minutes

  • Quorum: 1/3 of total strength or 2 directors (whichever is higher)
  • Meeting adjourned if quorum not present within 30 minutes
  • Minutes must be prepared within 30 days of the meeting
  • Minutes signed by the chairman or at the next meeting
  • Circulation of draft minutes to all directors
General Meetings

AGM & EGM Compliance

Section 96 and Section 100 of the Companies Act 2013 govern the convening and conduct of Annual General Meetings and Extraordinary General Meetings.

Annual General Meeting (AGM)

  • Mandatory for every company (except OPC and government companies)
  • Within 6 months from the end of financial year
  • Gap between two AGMs not more than 15 months
  • Notice of at least 21 clear days required
  • Business: approval of financial statements, dividend, auditor appointment, appointment/ratification of auditors

Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM)

  • Convened by the board on its own or on requisition by members
  • Members holding 1/10th of paid-up capital can requisition
  • Notice of at least 21 clear days required
  • Shorter notice allowed with consent of 95% members
  • For urgent matters requiring shareholder approval
Director Obligations

Director Compliance Under Companies Act

Directors have extensive obligations under the Companies Act 2013, including fiduciary duties, disclosure requirements, and disqualification criteria.

Director Identification & Appointment

DIN Requirement

Every person intending to be appointed as director must obtain a DIN (Section 153). DIN is applied through Form DIR-3 and is valid for the lifetime of the director.

Disqualification (Section 164)

A person cannot be appointed as director if: of unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, convicted by court, or if the company has not filed financial statements/annual returns for 3 continuous years.

Fiduciary Duties of Directors (Section 166)

Duty to Act in Good Faith

Directors must act in good faith in the best interest of the company, its employees, shareholders, community, and environment.

Duty of Care & Diligence

Directors must exercise due care, skill, and diligence as would be exercised by a reasonably diligent person with the same knowledge and experience.

Avoidance of Conflict of Interest

Directors must avoid situations where they have a direct or indirect interest that conflicts with the interest of the company.

No Unfair Gain

Directors shall not make any undue gain, either directly or indirectly, at the expense of the company.

Disclosure & Compliance Obligations

Interest in Contracts (Section 184)

Directors must disclose their interest in any contract or arrangement at the first board meeting of every financial year and whenever there is a change.

Related Party Transactions (Section 188)

Directors must ensure compliance with related party transaction provisions, including prior board/shareholder approval for specified transactions.

Annual KYC (DIR-3 KYC)

Every director must file DIR-3 KYC annually by September 30 to update personal and contact information with the MCA.

Board Committees

Directors must ensure constitution and compliance of board committees like Audit Committee, NRC, and CSR Committee as required by the Act and SEBI LODR.

Statutory Disclosures

Disclosure & Register Requirements

The Companies Act 2013 mandates several disclosure and register maintenance requirements for companies.

Statutory Registers

Companies must maintain registers of members, directors, charges, loans, and contracts. These must be kept at the registered office and updated in real-time.

Board Resolutions

All board resolutions must be documented in minutes books. Certain resolutions require filing with the ROC within 30 days in Form MGT-14.

Financial Disclosure

Companies must disclose related party transactions, director remuneration, loans to directors, and other specified information in financial statements and annual returns.

Event-Based Compliance

Event-Based Compliance Requirements

Certain corporate events trigger specific compliance obligations under the Companies Act that must be fulfilled within prescribed timelines.

Change in Directors

Appointment, resignation, or removal of directors requires board resolution, filing of Form DIR-12 with ROC within 30 days, and updating statutory registers.

Change in Authorized Capital

Increase in authorized capital requires special resolution, filing of Form SH-7 with ROC, and stamp duty payment where applicable.

Share Allotment

Issue of new shares requires board approval, allotment within 60 days, filing of Form PAS-3 with ROC, and updating register of members.

Change in MOA/AOA

Alteration of memorandum or articles requires special resolution, filing of Form MGT-14 with ROC, and updating statutory registers.

Creation of Charge

Creation of mortgage or charge on company assets requires board resolution, filing of Form CHG-1 with ROC within 30 days, and maintenance of register of charges.

Change in Registered Office

Shifting registered office requires board resolution, special resolution (for state-to-state shift), filing of Form INC-22/INC-23, and intimation to ROC.

Penalties

Penalties for Non-Compliance

The Companies Act 2013 prescribes stringent penalties for non-compliance with its provisions.

Company Penalties

Fine ranging from ₹25,000 to ₹25 lakhs depending on the nature of default. For continuing offences, additional daily fine may be imposed.

Officer Penalties

Officers in default may face fine ranging from ₹10,000 to ₹5 lakhs. In some cases, imprisonment may also be imposed for fraudulent conduct.

Director Disqualification

Directors may be disqualified for 5 years if the company fails to file financial statements or annual returns for 3 continuous financial years.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the board meeting requirements under the Companies Act 2013?

Under Section 173 of the Companies Act 2013, every company must hold a minimum of 4 board meetings every year with a gap of not more than 120 days between two consecutive meetings. The first board meeting must be held within 30 days of incorporation. Proper notice of at least 7 days must be given to all directors.

What is the quorum for a board meeting?

The quorum for a board meeting is one-third of the total strength of the board of directors or two directors, whichever is higher. A meeting at which a quorum is not present within 30 minutes from the time fixed for the meeting shall stand adjourned to the same day in the next week.

What is the maximum gap between two AGMs?

Under Section 96 of the Companies Act 2013, every company must hold an Annual General Meeting (AGM) within 6 months from the date of closing of the financial year. No two AGMs should have a gap of more than 15 months.

What are the disqualification criteria for directors?

Under Section 164 of the Companies Act 2013, a person is disqualified from being appointed as a director if: (a) he is of unsound mind, (b) he is an undischarged insolvent, (c) he has applied to be adjudicated insolvent and the application is pending, (d) he has been convicted by a court, or (e) the company has not filed financial statements or annual returns for a continuous period of 3 financial years.

What is the procedure for appointment of auditor?

The auditor is appointed at the first AGM of the company and holds office until the conclusion of the sixth AGM. Subsequent appointment is done at every AGM. Form ADT-1 must be filed with the ROC within 15 days of the AGM.
