LEI Registration in India - Complete Guide 2026

ComplianceBridge Advisory February 5, 2026 12 min read

Table of Contents

The Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) has become an essential compliance requirement for entities participating in financial transactions in India. With the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) progressively expanding the scope of entities required to obtain LEI, understanding the registration process, renewal requirements, and compliance obligations is critical for businesses. This comprehensive guide covers everything you need to know about LEI registration in India.

The LEI system was developed in response to the 2008 global financial crisis to improve transparency and accountability in financial markets. The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF) manages the global LEI system, while Local Operating Units (LOUs) in each country are responsible for issuing LEIs to legal entities. In India, the LEI system has been adopted by the RBI, SEBI, and other regulators to enhance the traceability and identification of entities in financial transactions.

The LEI System: An Overview

The Legal Entity Identifier is a 20-character alphanumeric code based on the ISO 17442 standard. It uniquely identifies legal entities that are involved in financial transactions globally. The LEI connects to key reference information that enables clear and unique identification of legal entities participating in financial transactions.

The global LEI system is managed by GLEIF, which was established in June 2014 by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) to support the implementation of the LEI system. GLEIF accredits Local Operating Units (LOUs) that issue LEIs to legal entities in their respective jurisdictions. In India, entities can obtain LEI from any GLEIF-accredited LOU that operates in India.

The LEI code itself contains embedded information about the entity, including the entity name, jurisdiction of incorporation, legal form, and registration number. The reference data associated with the LEI is publicly available through the GLEIF database, enabling transparency and comparability across jurisdictions.

What is LEI and Why is it Important

The LEI is a unique global identifier for legal entities that participate in financial transactions. It provides a standardized method of identifying parties to financial transactions across jurisdictions, reducing systemic risk and improving regulatory oversight. The LEI is a 20-character code that is unique to each legal entity and does not change over time, even if the entity undergoes structural changes.

For entities in India, the LEI is particularly important because it is required for participation in certain financial markets and transactions. The RBI has mandated LEI for entities entering into OTC derivatives contracts, and has progressively expanded the scope to include entities with significant exposure to regulated entities. SEBI has also introduced LEI requirements for certain market participants.

The importance of LEI extends beyond regulatory compliance. Having an LEI enhances an entity's credibility and transparency in financial markets, facilitates cross-border transactions, and enables better risk management by counterparties and regulators. Many financial institutions and market participants prefer to transact only with entities that have a valid LEI.

Who Needs LEI in India

The RBI and SEBI have mandated LEI for various categories of entities in India. The scope of mandatory LEI registration has been progressively expanded over the years.

Entities Required to Obtain LEI:

  • OTC Derivative Participants: All entities entering into over-the-counter (OTC) derivative contracts must have a valid LEI as per RBI directions.
  • Large Borrowers: Entities with aggregate exposure of Rs 10 crore or more to RBI-regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, etc.) must obtain LEI.
  • Corporate Bond Issuers: Entities issuing corporate bonds must have a valid LEI for ISIN creation and bond issuance.
  • SEBI-Regulated Entities: Certain entities regulated by SEBI, including mutual funds and portfolio managers, may require LEI for specific transactions.
  • Cross-Border Transactions: Entities involved in cross-border financial transactions may require LEI as per international regulatory requirements.

LEI Registration Process in India

The LEI registration process in India involves applying to a GLEIF-accredited Local Operating Unit (LOU). The process is relatively straightforward but requires careful documentation and verification.

Step-by-Step Registration Process:

  • Step 1: Select an LOU: Choose a GLEIF-accredited LOU that operates in India. Multiple LOUs are available, and entities can select based on their specific requirements.
  • Step 2: Submit Application: Complete the LEI application form with accurate entity details including legal name, registration number, jurisdiction, and authorized signatory information.
  • Step 3: Provide Supporting Documents: Submit documents such as certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, board resolution authorizing the application, and KYC documents of authorized signatories.
  • Step 4: Verification: The LOU verifies the submitted information against public records and regulatory databases. This may involve cross-referencing with the ROC database, RBI records, or other regulatory sources.
  • Step 5: LEI Issuance: Upon successful verification, the LOU issues a 20-character LEI code. The LEI is recorded in the global GLEIF database and is publicly accessible.

The entire registration process typically takes 3-7 business days, depending on the LOU and the completeness of the application. Some LOUs offer expedited processing for an additional fee. The registration fee varies across LOUs and typically ranges from Rs 3,000 to Rs 10,000 for initial registration.

LEI Renewal Requirements

LEI is not a one-time registration. It must be renewed annually to ensure that the entity's information in the GLEIF database remains current and accurate. The renewal process involves updating the entity's reference data and paying the annual renewal fee.

The renewal must be completed before the expiry date of the LEI. If the LEI is not renewed before the expiry date, it will be marked as lapsed in the GLEIF database. A lapsed LEI is treated as if the entity does not have an LEI, and the entity will not be able to participate in financial transactions that require LEI.

If the LEI has lapsed, the entity must undergo the full registration process again, including payment of the initial registration fee. To avoid disruption, entities should track their LEI renewal dates and initiate the renewal process well in advance. Many LOUs send renewal reminders to the registered email address before the expiry date.

RBI LEI Requirements and Directions

The Reserve Bank of India has issued several circulars and directions mandating LEI for various categories of transactions and entities. These requirements have been progressively expanded to cover a wider range of financial activities.

Key RBI Directions on LEI:

  • RBI/2014-15/249: Mandatory LEI for all participants in OTC derivative markets. Entities without LEI cannot enter into new OTC derivative contracts.
  • RBI/2016-17/153: LEI mandatory for entities with aggregate exposure of Rs 5 crore or more to banks (later enhanced to Rs 10 crore).
  • RBI/2022-23/56: Further expansion of LEI requirements for various categories of financial transactions and reporting.
  • LEI for CBDC and Digital Transactions: LEI requirements for central bank digital currency (CBDC) transactions and other digital financial instruments.

The RBI requires regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, etc.) to obtain LEI from their borrowers who meet the threshold criteria. The regulated entity must report the LEI of the borrower to the RBI in the prescribed format. Failure to comply with LEI requirements may result in the entity being unable to access credit facilities or participate in certain financial transactions.

GLEIF Framework and Global LEI System

The Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF) is the non-profit organization that oversees the global LEI system. GLEIF was established by the Financial Stability Board (FSB) in June 2014 to support the implementation of the LEI system as recommended by the G20 leaders.

GLEIF accredits and monitors Local Operating Units (LOUs) that issue LEIs to legal entities. As of 2026, there are over 40 accredited LOUs worldwide, with several operating in India. GLEIF maintains the global LEI database, which contains the reference data of all entities that have been assigned an LEI.

The GLEIF database is publicly accessible and provides search functionality to look up LEI codes and associated entity information. This transparency enables market participants, regulators, and other stakeholders to verify the identity of legal entities involved in financial transactions. The GLEIF also publishes quarterly reports on the state of the global LEI system, including statistics on LEI issuance, renewal rates, and coverage across jurisdictions.

LEI vs CIN: Key Differences

Many business owners and compliance professionals confuse LEI with CIN (Corporate Identity Number). While both are identification numbers for companies, they serve different purposes and are governed by different regulatory frameworks.

Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)

  • 20-character alphanumeric code
  • Issued by GLEIF-accredited LOUs
  • Global identification standard (ISO 17442)
  • Required for financial transactions
  • Must be renewed annually
  • Managed by GLEIF globally

Corporate Identity Number (CIN)

  • 21-digit alphanumeric code
  • Assigned by Registrar of Companies (ROC)
  • India-specific identifier under Companies Act
  • Required for all Indian companies
  • Permanent - does not expire
  • Managed by MCA in India

Frequently Asked Questions

What is LEI and who needs it in India?

LEI (Legal Entity Identifier) is a 20-character unique identifier assigned to legal entities participating in financial transactions. In India, entities involved in OTC derivatives, corporate bonds, and entities borrowing Rs 10 crore or more from RBI-regulated entities are required to obtain LEI.

How do I register for LEI in India?

LEI registration in India is done through Local Operating Units (LOUs) authorized by GLEIF. The entity must submit an application with supporting documents including certificate of incorporation, board resolution, and KYC documents. The LOU verifies the information and issues the LEI code within 3-7 business days.

How often does LEI need to be renewed?

LEI must be renewed annually. The renewal process involves updating the entity information and paying the renewal fee. If the LEI is not renewed before the expiry date, it will be marked as lapsed and the entity will need to undergo the full registration process again.

What is the RBI LEI requirement?

The RBI has mandated that all entities entering into OTC derivatives contracts must have an LEI. Additionally, entities with aggregate exposure of Rs 10 crore or more to RBI-regulated entities must obtain LEI. Entities without LEI cannot enter into new derivative contracts or access credit facilities above the threshold.

What is the difference between LEI and CIN?

CIN (Corporate Identity Number) is a 21-digit alphanumeric identifier assigned by the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act, 2013, and is specific to Indian companies. LEI is a 20-character identifier used globally for financial transaction reporting and is issued by GLEIF-authorized LOUs. CIN identifies a company for regulatory purposes in India, while LEI identifies entities for global financial transaction tracking.
