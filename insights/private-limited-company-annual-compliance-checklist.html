Private Limited Company Annual Compliance Checklist India 2026

ComplianceBridge Advisory January 15, 2026 12 min read

Table of Contents

Every private limited company registered in India under the Companies Act, 2013 is subject to a comprehensive set of annual compliance obligations. Missing even a single filing deadline can attract penalties, director disqualification, and adverse regulatory consequences. This guide provides a complete, actionable checklist for directors, company secretaries, and CFOs managing compliance for private limited companies.

The compliance landscape for private limited companies has evolved considerably since the enactment of the Companies Act, 2013. With the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) continuously updating filing requirements, introducing new forms, and enhancing the MCA21 portal, staying current with obligations is not merely a regulatory formality — it is a business imperative. This comprehensive checklist is designed to serve as a definitive reference for company secretaries, CFOs, directors, and business owners who need to ensure full compliance throughout the calendar year.

Understanding the Annual Compliance Framework

The annual compliance framework for private limited companies in India is governed primarily by the Companies Act, 2013, the Companies (Management and Administration) Rules, 2014, and the Companies (Accounts) Rules, 2014. These regulations prescribe a series of filings, meetings, and maintenance requirements that must be completed within specific timeframes.

The compliance obligations can be broadly categorized into several key areas: annual return filings with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), financial statement preparation and filing, board and general meeting compliance, director-related obligations, statutory register maintenance, and tax-related filings. Each category has its own set of forms, deadlines, and penalty provisions that companies must navigate carefully.

For private limited companies, the compliance burden is somewhat lighter than for public listed companies, as they are not subject to SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations. However, the requirements under the Companies Act remain substantial, and non-compliance carries serious consequences including monetary penalties, director disqualification, and even prosecution in extreme cases.

Financial Year and Annual General Meeting

Every company in India must maintain its books of account on an accrual basis and follow the double-entry system of accounting. The financial year for all Indian companies runs from April 1 to March 31, regardless of the date of incorporation. Companies are required to close their books of account at the end of each financial year and prepare financial statements that include the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash flow statement, and notes to accounts.

The Annual General Meeting (AGM) is a mandatory requirement under Section 96 of the Companies Act, 2013. Every company must hold its first AGM within nine months from the close of the first financial year following incorporation. Subsequent AGMs must be held within six months from the close of the financial year — that is, by September 30 of each year. At least 21 days' clear notice must be given to all members before the AGM, and the notice must include the date, time, place, and agenda of the meeting.

The AGM serves as the forum where shareholders approve the financial statements, declare dividends (if applicable), appoint or reappoint auditors, and transact any other business that requires shareholder approval. The minutes of the AGM must be recorded and maintained in the minute book within 30 days of the meeting.

Board Meeting Requirements

Section 173 of the Companies Act, 2013 mandates that every company must hold a minimum of four board meetings in a calendar year, with a gap of not more than 120 days between two consecutive meetings. For newly incorporated companies, the first board meeting must be held within 30 days of incorporation.

The quorum for a board meeting is one-third of the total strength of the board of directors or two directors, whichever is higher. A valid notice of at least seven days must be given to all directors, either in writing or through electronic means. The notice must specify the date, time, place, and agenda of the meeting.

Key matters that must be transacted at board meetings include approval of financial statements, declaration of dividends, appointment of key managerial personnel, approval of related party transactions, and review of compliance reports. The minutes of every board meeting must be recorded in the minute book within 30 days of the conclusion of the meeting.

Annual Return Filing (MGT-7 / MGT-7A)

Under Section 92 of the Companies Act, 2013, every company must file an annual return with the Registrar of Companies within 60 days from the date of the AGM. The form to be used depends on the company's paid-up capital and turnover:

  • MGT-7: Applicable to companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more, or turnover of ₹50 crore or more. This is a detailed form requiring extensive disclosures about share capital, members, directors, and meetings.
  • MGT-7A: Applicable to One Person Companies (OPCs) and small companies as defined under Section 2(85) of the Companies Act. This is a simplified version with fewer disclosure requirements.

The annual return must be signed by the company secretary in practice, or where there is no company secretary, by the director of the company. The return must be filed electronically on the MCA21 portal and must be accompanied by the prescribed filing fee.

Key information required in the annual return includes details of the registered office, principal business activities, particulars of members and debenture holders, details of shares held by or on behalf of the Foreign Institutional Investors, turnover and net worth details, and compliance with Sections 186 and 188 of the Companies Act.

Financial Statement Filing (AOC-4)

Section 137 of the Companies Act, 2013 requires every company to file a copy of its financial statements and related documents with the Registrar of Companies. The prescribed form is AOC-4, which must be filed within 30 days from the date of the AGM.

The documents that must be filed along with AOC-4 include the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash flow statement, notes to accounts, Board's report, Auditors' report, and the Declaration of Independence by the auditor. For companies covered under Section 134(3)(c) of the Companies Act (i.e., companies with paid-up capital of ₹5 crore or more, or turnover of ₹100 crore or more), the financial statements must be prepared in XBRL format using the AOC-4 XBRL form.

Small companies and OPCs have the option to file financial statements in AOC-4 CFS, which is a simplified consolidated form. The financial statements must comply with the Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) applicable to the company, and the auditor's report must be in the prescribed format under Schedule VII of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014.

DIR-3 KYC — Director KYC Compliance

Every individual who holds a Director Identification Number (DIN) as on March 31 of a financial year must file the DIR-3 KYC form with the Registrar of Companies. This is an annual requirement under Rule 12A of the Companies (Appointment and Qualification of Directors) Rules, 2014.

The DIR-3 KYC must be filed by September 30 of each year. The form requires the director to verify their personal details, including name, date of birth, address, PAN, Aadhaar, passport (if applicable), and contact details. The form must be digitally signed and can be submitted through the MCA21 portal.

For DINs that have been deactivated due to non-filing of DIR-3 KYC, the director must file the KYC form along with a late filing fee of ₹5,000. If the DIN remains deactivated for three consecutive years, it is liable to be deactivated permanently, and the director may face disqualification under Section 164(2)(a) of the Companies Act.

Statutory Register Maintenance

The Companies Act, 2013 requires every company to maintain several statutory registers at its registered office. These registers must be kept up to date and must be available for inspection by members and the Registrar as required under the Act.

Key Statutory Registers:

  • Register of Members (Section 88)
  • Register of Directors and Key Managerial Personnel (Section 170)
  • Register of Charges (Section 85)
  • Register of Loans, Guarantees, and Investments (Section 186)
  • Register of Contracts with Related Parties (Section 189)
  • Register of Investments (Section 187)
  • Register of Renewed and Duplicate Share Certificates

Director-Related Compliance Obligations

Beyond DIR-3 KYC, directors of private limited companies have several other compliance obligations. These include disclosure of interest in other entities under Section 184, filing of MBP-1 (Disclosure of Interest by Director), and compliance with Section 164 regarding disqualification grounds.

Every director must disclose their interest in other entities at the first board meeting of the financial year or upon acquiring any interest, whichever is later. The disclosure must be made in Form MBP-1 and must be placed before the board of directors. Failure to disclose interest is an offense that can attract a penalty of up to ₹5 lakh.

Directors must also comply with the provisions regarding resignation and vacation of office. A director who wishes to resign must file Form DIR-11 with the ROC within 30 days of resignation. If a director's DIN is deactivated or if they fail to attend board meetings for 12 consecutive months, they may be deemed to have vacated office under Section 167.

Other Important Annual Filings

In addition to the core filings discussed above, private limited companies must also comply with several other filing requirements:

  • ADT-1 (Auditor Appointment): Must be filed within 15 days of the AGM if a new auditor is appointed. The first auditor is appointed by the board within 30 days of incorporation.
  • MSME-1: Half-yearly return of outstanding payments to micro and small enterprises, due within 31 days from the end of each half year (September 30 and March 31).
  • DPT-3: Annual return of deposits and transactions not considered as deposits, due by June 30 of each year.
  • BEN-2 (Significant Beneficial Owners): Return of significant beneficial owners, due within 30 days of receiving declaration from beneficial owners.
  • Income Tax Returns: Due by July 31 (for companies not requiring audit) or October 31 (for companies requiring tax audit) of each assessment year.

Penalties for Non-Compliance

The Companies Act, 2013 prescribes severe penalties for non-compliance with annual filing requirements. Understanding these penalties is essential for directors and company secretaries to prioritize timely compliance.

For late filing of annual returns (MGT-7/MGT-7A) and financial statements (AOC-4), the penalty is ₹100 per day for OPCs and small companies, and ₹200 per day for other companies. There is no upper limit on the penalty for annual return filings, which means the penalty can accumulate indefinitely until the form is filed.

If a company fails to file its annual return or financial statements for three consecutive financial years, the directors of the company face disqualification under Section 164(2)(a) of the Companies Act. This disqualification applies for a period of five years from the date on which the third consecutive financial year was due for filing.

The MCA has also introduced compounding of offenses for certain filing defaults, which allows companies to remedy defaults by paying a compounding fee. However, this is not a substitute for timely compliance and should only be used as a remedial measure.

Master Annual Compliance Checklist

The following comprehensive checklist summarizes all the key compliance obligations and their deadlines for a private limited company:

APR
File DPT-3 (Return of Deposits) by June 30. Hold first board meeting within 30 days of incorporation for new companies.
JUL
File Income Tax Returns by July 31 (non-audit cases). File ADT-1 (Auditor Appointment) within 15 days of AGM.
SEP
Hold AGM by September 30. File AOC-4 within 30 days of AGM. File MGT-7/MGT-7A within 60 days of AGM. File DIR-3 KYC by September 30. File MSME-1 (half-yearly) by October 31.
OCT
File Income Tax Returns by October 31 (audit cases). File MSME-1 by October 31.
DEC
Hold two board meetings (minimum gap of 120 days). Update statutory registers. Review compliance status for the year.
MAR
Close books of account. Prepare financial statements. File MSME-1 (half-yearly) by March 31. File DIR-11 (if director resigns).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the annual compliance requirements for a private limited company in India?

The annual compliance requirements include filing of annual returns (MGT-7/MGT-7A), financial statements (AOC-4), DIR-3 KYC for directors, holding board meetings (minimum 4 per year), holding the annual general meeting (AGM), maintaining statutory registers, and filing income tax returns. The specific forms and deadlines depend on the company's size and turnover.

What is the due date for filing annual returns for a private limited company?

The annual return (MGT-7/MGT-7A) must be filed within 60 days from the date of the AGM. For most companies, the AGM must be held by September 30, making the typical deadline for filing the annual return November 29. The financial statements (AOC-4) must be filed within 30 days from the AGM — typically by October 30.

What is the penalty for late filing of ROC returns?

The penalty for late filing of ROC returns is ₹100 per day for small companies and OPCs, and ₹200 per day for other companies. There is no upper limit on the penalty for annual return filings. Additionally, directors may face disqualification if returns are not filed for a continuous period of 3 financial years.

Do all private limited companies need to hold an AGM?

Yes, all private limited companies registered in India are required to hold an Annual General Meeting under Section 96 of the Companies Act, 2013. The AGM must be held within 6 months from the close of the financial year, and at least 21 days' notice must be given to all members. There is no exemption for small private companies.

What is the difference between MGT-7 and MGT-7A?

MGT-7 is the annual return form applicable to companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more, or turnover of ₹50 crore or more. MGT-7A is a simplified version applicable to OPCs and small companies as defined under Section 2(85) of the Companies Act, 2013. Both forms must be filed within 60 days of the AGM.
