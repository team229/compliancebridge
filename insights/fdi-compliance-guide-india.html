FDI Compliance Guide for Foreign Investors in India 2026

ComplianceBridge Advisory January 20, 2026 14 min read

Table of Contents

India remains one of the most attractive destinations for foreign direct investment (FDI) globally, with FDI inflows consistently exceeding $80 billion annually in recent years. For foreign investors looking to establish or expand operations in India, understanding the FDI compliance framework is essential. This guide provides a comprehensive overview of FDI routes, filing requirements, pricing guidelines, and ongoing compliance obligations.

The regulatory framework governing FDI in India is administered jointly by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), and the concerned administrative ministries. The Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999, and the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 form the primary legal basis for FDI regulations. Understanding these regulations is critical for foreign investors to ensure compliant investment structuring and avoid penalties.

Understanding the FDI Framework in India

Foreign Direct Investment in India is defined as investment made by a person resident outside India in an Indian company through equity instruments. The term "equity instruments" includes equity shares, comp convertible debentures, fully and partly convertible preference shares, and share warrants, as well as stock equivalent instruments such as American Depository Receipts (ADRs), Global Depository Receipts (GDRs), and fully convertible bonds.

The FDI policy is periodically updated by the DPIIT through Press Notes issued under the FDI policy framework. These policy changes are operationalized through the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, issued by the Ministry of Finance. The RBI also issues circulars and master directions to provide operational guidance on FEMA compliance.

FDI in India is permitted under two broad routes: the Automatic Route and the Government Route. Under the Automatic Route, foreign investors do not require prior approval from the RBI or any government ministry. The investee company must only comply with sectoral caps and other conditions specified in the FDI policy and FEMA rules. Under the Government Route, prior approval from the concerned ministry or department is required, and the application must be filed through the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP).

FDI Routes: Automatic vs Government Route

The distinction between the Automatic Route and the Government Route is fundamental to understanding FDI compliance in India. The majority of sectors in India now permit 100% FDI under the Automatic Route, making India one of the most open economies for foreign investment.

Automatic Route — Key Features:

  • No prior government or RBI approval required
  • Investee company must file Form FC-GPR within 30 days of share allotment
  • Applicable to most sectors including IT, manufacturing, services, and infrastructure
  • Subject to sectoral caps and other conditions as specified in the FDI policy

Government Route — Key Features:

  • Prior approval from the concerned ministry or department required
  • Application filed online through the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP)
  • Applicable to sensitive sectors like defence, telecom, media, and multi-brand retail
  • Approval timeline varies based on the sector and involved ministries

Certain sectors and activities are either prohibited for FDI or have specific conditions attached. These include multi-brand retail (subject to conditions), defence (subject to FIPB-like approval for certain countries), and sectors reserved for public sector enterprises. The FDI policy also prohibits investment from countries sharing a land border with India without prior government approval, which applies to investments from Pakistan, Bangladesh, and other neighboring countries.

Pricing Guidelines for FDI

The pricing of shares issued to foreign investors is governed by the pricing guidelines issued by the Department of Economic Affairs (DEA) under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019. Compliance with these pricing guidelines is essential to avoid FEMA violations.

Pricing for Listed Companies:

The price of shares issued to a person resident outside India must not be less than the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of the equity shares of the company during the two weeks preceding the relevant date (i.e., the date of the board meeting or general meeting at which the issue is approved).

Pricing for Unlisted Companies:

The price of shares must be determined by a Chartered Accountant or a merchant banker using the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) methodology or any other internationally accepted pricing methodology. The fair value must be certified by a Chartered Accountant, and the shares must not be issued at a price lower than the fair value so determined. The valuation certificate must be issued within 90 days of the relevant date.

Form FC-GPR — Filing for Fresh Investment

Form FC-GPR (Foreign Currency Gross Provisional Return) is the primary form used for reporting foreign investment in an Indian company. This form must be filed when a foreign investor makes a fresh investment in an Indian company by acquiring shares. The form is filed online through the FIRMS portal of the RBI.

The FC-GPR must be filed within 30 days from the date of allotment of shares. The filing must be accompanied by supporting documents including the board resolution approving the share allotment, the share allotment certificate, the valuation certificate (for unlisted companies), and the certificate from the company secretary certifying compliance with FEMA rules.

Key information required in the FC-GPR form includes details of the foreign investor, the Indian company, the investment amount, the sector of activity, the FDI route (automatic or government), and the post-issue shareholding pattern. The form must be digitally signed by an authorized signatory of the Indian company.

Form FC-TRS — Transfer of Shares

Form FC-TRS (Foreign Currency Transfer of Shares) is used for reporting the transfer of shares between a person resident in India and a person resident outside India. This form must be filed when shares are transferred from a resident to a non-resident or vice versa.

The FC-TRS must be filed within 60 days from the date of transfer of shares. The filing must be accompanied by the board resolution of the transferor and transferee companies, the share transfer deed, the valuation certificate (if applicable), and other supporting documents.

For listed companies, the transfer must be reported on the stock exchange platform, and the FC-TRS must be filed within 60 days of the settlement of the trade. For unlisted companies, the transfer must comply with the pricing guidelines and the requirements of the Companies Act, 2013.

Downstream Investment Compliance

When an Indian company with foreign investment (either directly or indirectly) makes further investment in another Indian company, this is termed as a downstream investment. The downstream investee company is treated as a company with foreign investment for the purposes of FEMA compliance.

The downstream investing company must report the investment in Form FC-TRS with the RBI within 30 days of the transfer. Additionally, the downstream investee company must file an annual return of its foreign investment details with the RBI. The downstream investment must comply with sectoral caps and entry conditions applicable to foreign investment in the relevant sector.

The concept of downstream investment is particularly important for Indian companies that have received foreign venture capital investment or foreign direct investment. Such companies, when making further domestic investments, must ensure that the downstream investee company complies with all applicable FEMA regulations and FDI policy requirements.

Ongoing Reporting Requirements

Foreign investment in Indian companies triggers several ongoing reporting obligations. These must be monitored continuously to ensure compliance throughout the lifecycle of the investment.

  • Annual Compliance Certificate: The company secretary of the investee company must issue an annual compliance certificate certifying compliance with FEMA rules and FDI policy conditions.
  • Annual Return on Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA): Companies that have received foreign investment must file the FLA return with the RBI by July 15 of each year.
  • Annual Performance Report: Companies that have made overseas direct investment must file an annual performance report with the RBI by December 31 of each year.
  • Return of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs): Companies that have issued FCCBs must file returns with the RBI as per the prescribed timeline.

Prohibited and Restricted Sectors for FDI

While India is generally open to foreign investment, certain sectors and activities are either completely prohibited or subject to additional restrictions under the FDI policy. Foreign investors must carefully review sectoral conditions before making investment decisions.

Prohibited Sectors:

  • Lottery business, gambling and betting
  • Chit funds and Nidhi companies
  • Trading in transferable development rights (TDRs)
  • Real estate business (except construction development)
  • Tobacco manufacturing
  • Atomic energy
  • Sectors reserved for public sector enterprises (e.g., railways, atomic energy)

Penalties for FEMA Non-Compliance

Non-compliance with FDI regulations and FEMA requirements can attract severe penalties. Under Section 13 of FEMA, contravention can result in a penalty up to three times the sum involved in the contravention, or up to ₹2 lakh where the amount is not quantifiable. For continuing contravention, an additional penalty of ₹5,000 per day may be imposed.

The RBI has also introduced compounding of offenses for certain FEMA violations, which allows parties to remedy defaults by paying a compounding fee. However, this is not a substitute for timely compliance and is available only for cases where the contravention is not wilful or fraudulent.

Foreign investors should also be aware of the reporting deadlines. Late filing of FC-GPR or FC-TRS attracts additional penalties, and the RBI may also initiate adjudication proceedings in serious cases of non-compliance. It is advisable to engage experienced FEMA compliance professionals to ensure timely and accurate filing of all required forms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between automatic route and government route for FDI in India?

Under the automatic route, foreign investors do not require prior approval from the government or RBI for investment. The investee company must only file the FC-GPR form with the RBI within 30 days of share allotment. Under the government route, prior approval from the concerned ministry or department is required before making the investment.

What is the FC-GPR form and when is it filed?

Form FC-GPR is filed when a foreign investor makes a fresh investment in an Indian company by acquiring shares. It must be filed within 30 days from the date of allotment of shares. The form is filed online through the FIRMS portal of the RBI and must be accompanied by supporting documents.

What is the pricing guideline for FDI in India?

For listed companies, the price must not be less than the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) of the shares during the two weeks preceding the relevant date. For unlisted companies, the price must be determined by a Chartered Accountant or merchant banker based on Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) methodology.

What sectors are prohibited for FDI in India?

FDI is prohibited in lottery business, gambling and betting, chit funds, Nidhi companies, trading in TDRs, real estate business (except construction development), tobacco manufacturing, and atomic energy.

What are the downstream investment reporting requirements?

When an Indian company with foreign investment makes further investment in another Indian company, it must file Form FC-TRS with the RBI within 30 days of the transfer. The downstream investee company must also report the investment in its annual return to the RBI.
