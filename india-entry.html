Entry Structures

Modes of Entry into India

Foreign companies can establish their presence in India through several structures, each designed for different business objectives and regulatory requirements.

Wholly-Owned Subsidiary (Private Limited Company)

Most Popular

A wholly-owned subsidiary is the most common entry structure for foreign companies in India. Under this structure, the foreign parent company holds 100% of the shares of an Indian Private Limited Company. This structure offers full operational control, limited liability protection, and the ability to scale operations independently. It is governed by the Companies Act 2013 and requires compliance with FDI regulations under FEMA.

  • 100% foreign ownership allowed in most sectors under automatic route
  • Limited liability protection for the parent company
  • Separate legal entity with perpetual succession
  • Can raise equity funding from Indian and international investors
  • Requires minimum 2 directors (at least 1 Indian resident)

Branch Office (BO)

A Branch Office is an extension of the foreign parent company in India. It can undertake activities permitted by the RBI, including export/import of goods, rendering of professional services, and research activities. Branch offices require RBI approval and are subject to specific compliance requirements including annual activity certificates and financial reporting to the RBI.

  • Extension of the foreign parent — not a separate legal entity
  • Requires RBI approval for establishment
  • Cannot undertake manufacturing/trading activities directly
  • Annual activity certificate from statutory auditors
  • Profits can be repatriated after tax compliance

Liaison Office (LO)

A Liaison Office is suitable for foreign companies exploring the Indian market or maintaining communication with Indian customers and suppliers. It cannot undertake any commercial, trading, or industrial activity directly. Its role is limited to liaison activities, market research, and facilitating communication between the parent company and Indian business partners.

  • Non-commercial activities only — liaison and coordination
  • Requires RBI approval (via AD Category-I bank)
  • No revenue generation allowed
  • Annual compliance certificate required
  • Ideal for market research and exploratory activities

Project Office (PO)

A Project Office is established by a foreign company to execute a specific project in India. It is a temporary structure that ceases to exist upon completion of the project. Project offices are commonly used for infrastructure projects, turnkey projects, or specific assignments awarded by Indian entities or government agencies.

  • Temporary structure for specific project execution
  • RBI approval not required if funded by inward remittance or EPCG
  • Cannot undertake other commercial activities
  • Ceases upon project completion
Regulatory Framework

FDI Regulations & FEMA Compliance

Understanding the regulatory framework governing foreign investment in India is critical for a successful market entry.

FDI Routes

Automatic Route

100% FDI is permitted under the automatic route in most sectors without prior government or RBI approval. The investee company only needs to notify the RBI within 30 days of receiving inward remittance and file Form FC-GPR for allotment of shares.

Government Route

FDI in certain sectors requires prior approval from the concerned ministry or department. Applications are filed through the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP) or the DPIIT portal. Sectors like defense, insurance, media, and multi-brand retail have specific conditions.

Key FEMA Compliance Requirements

  • FC-GPR: Filing for allotment of shares to foreign investors
  • FC-TRS: Transfer of shares between resident and non-resident
  • ECB Returns: Monthly and annual returns for external commercial borrowings
  • Annual Performance Report: Annual filing with RBI for BO/LO
  • Downstream Investment: Reporting of indirect foreign investment
  • Valuation Compliance: fair market value determination for share issuances

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best structure for foreign companies entering India?

The best structure depends on the business objectives. A wholly-owned subsidiary (Private Limited Company) is ideal for full control and scalable operations. A branch office is suitable for specific projects. A liaison office is appropriate for exploratory activities. We assess your requirements and recommend the most suitable structure.

Do foreign companies need RBI approval to set up in India?

RBI approval is required for certain structures like branch offices and liaison offices in specific sectors. For wholly-owned subsidiaries in automatic route sectors, no prior RBI/FIPB approval is needed. However, all foreign investments must be reported to the RBI and comply with FEMA regulations.

What is the FDI policy in India?

India allows 100% FDI under the automatic route in most sectors. Some sectors require government approval (formerly FIPB, now concerned ministries). FDI is governed by the FDI Policy, FEMA regulations, and RBI Master Directions. Sector-specific caps and conditions apply in certain industries like defense, insurance, and media.

How long does it take to set up a subsidiary in India?

Setting up a wholly-owned subsidiary typically takes 4-6 weeks, including name approval, incorporation, PAN/TAN, bank account opening, and post-incorporation registrations. The timeline may vary based on MCA processing and sector-specific approvals required.

What are the reporting requirements for foreign investment in India?

Foreign companies must report all investment transactions to the RBI through the AD bank. Key filings include FC-GPR (for fresh shares), FC-TRS (for transfer of shares), ECB returns, and annual performance reports. Non-compliance attracts penalties under FEMA.
