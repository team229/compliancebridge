Foreign Direct Investment Compliance in India

Comprehensive FDI compliance services for inbound foreign investment. Expert FC-GPR filing, FC-TRS filing, downstream investment compliance, and FDI structuring advisory.

Overview

End-to-End FDI Compliance & Advisory Services

India is one of the most attractive destinations for foreign direct investment, driven by its large market, young demographics, liberalized FDI policy, and growing integration with the global economy. The FDI policy framework in India is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), the FDI policy issued by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), and various RBI Master Directions and circulars. FDI can flow into India through two routes — the automatic route, where no prior government approval is required, and the government route, where prior approval from the concerned ministry or department is mandated.

FDI compliance involves multiple layers of regulatory requirements — from pricing guidelines and sectoral caps to reporting obligations and post-transaction compliance. FDI Routes

Automatic Route vs Government Route

Understanding the difference between the two FDI routes is critical for structuring your investment correctly and avoiding regulatory complications.

Automatic Route

Under the automatic route, foreign investment can be made without prior approval from the RBI or any government ministry. The investor or the Indian company simply needs to comply with the reporting requirements and sectoral conditions. Most sectors in India are now under the automatic route, making it the most common pathway for FDI.

  • No prior government approval required
  • Post-transaction reporting to RBI
  • Sectoral caps and conditions apply
  • Faster execution timeline

Government Route

Under the government route, prior approval from the concerned ministry or department is required before making the investment. This route applies to sectors that are restricted or have specific policy considerations. The application is filed through the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP), and approval is granted based on the sectoral policy and conditions.

  • Prior approval from ministry required
  • Filed through FIFP portal
  • Applies to restricted sectors
  • Longer processing timeline
Key Filings

FC-GPR Filing Requirements

FC-GPR is the primary form for reporting FDI in Indian companies. Timely and accurate filing is essential to maintain compliance.

01

When is FC-GPR Filing Required?

FC-GPR must be filed within 30 days of allotment of shares in the following cases: (a) Allotment of shares to a non-resident under the FDI scheme, (b) Conversion of external commercial borrowings (ECB) into equity, (c) Conversion of foreign currency convertible bonds (FCCBs) into equity, (d) Allotment of shares under the scheme of arrangement approved by NCLT, and (e) Issue of equity shares under the swap ratio in a scheme of merger or amalgamation. The form must be filed through the AD Category-I bank and is submitted on the FIRMS portal of the RBI.

02

Documents Required for FC-GPR

The following documents must be submitted along with the FC-GPR form: (a) Copy of the board resolution allotting shares, (b) Copy of the shareholder agreement, (c) Certificate from the Company Secretary certifying compliance with FDI conditions, (d) valuation certificate from a registered valuer (for unlisted companies), (e) share allotment certificate, (f) FLA details if applicable, and (g) any other document as may be required by the AD bank. All documents must be properly certified and submitted through the designated AD bank.

03

Consequences of Late FC-GPR Filing

Late filing of FC-GPR attracts compounding proceedings before the RBI. The compounding amount is determined based on the amount involved and the delay period. In addition to the compounding amount, the late filing may also result in complications during future fundraising, exit transactions, or regulatory inspections. It is therefore critical to ensure timely filing of FC-GPR within the 30-day deadline.

Share Transfer Compliance

FC-TRS Filing Requirements

FC-TRS is required when shares are transferred between a resident and a non-resident, or vice versa.

Inbound Transfer (Non-Resident to Resident)

When a non-resident transfers shares of an Indian company to a resident, FC-TRS must be filed within 60 days from the date of transfer. The transfer must comply with the pricing guidelines — the shares must be priced at fair market value as determined by a registered valuer. The buyer must also comply with the KYC requirements and the source of funds must be established.

  • Due within 60 days of transfer
  • Fair market value pricing required
  • KYC of buyer required

Outbound Transfer (Resident to Non-Resident)

When a resident transfers shares of an Indian company to a non-resident, FC-TRS must be filed within 60 days from the date of transfer. The transfer must comply with the FDI pricing guidelines, and the seller must ensure that the transaction is in compliance with the sectoral cap and other conditions applicable to the FDI route under which the original investment was made.

  • Due within 60 days of transfer
  • Sectoral cap compliance required
  • Pricing guidelines must be followed
Downstream Investment

Downstream Investment Compliance

Downstream investment refers to investment by an Indian company with FDI in another Indian company, subject to specific compliance requirements.

01

What Constitutes Downstream Investment?

Downstream investment occurs when an Indian company that has received FDI makes an investment in another Indian company (whether by way of equity, compulsorily convertible debentures, or compulsorily convertible preference shares). This creates a chain of foreign investment that must be tracked and reported to the RBI. The downstream entity is treated as an entity with indirect foreign investment and must comply with the same sectoral caps and conditionalities that apply to the parent entity.

02

Reporting Requirements for Downstream Investment

The Indian company making the downstream investment must report the investment to the RBI through the designated AD bank. The reporting must be completed within the prescribed timeline, and the downstream entity must maintain all records supporting the compliance. The downstream entity must also ensure that its activities fall within the permitted sectoral caps and that any conditions attached to the original FDI are met.

03

Compliance Framework for Downstream Entities

Downstream entities must establish a robust compliance framework that includes tracking the foreign investment chain, maintaining records of all upstream and downstream investments, ensuring compliance with sectoral caps, and filing all required returns with the RBI. Our team helps downstream entities set up this framework and provides ongoing compliance support to ensure continued adherence to regulatory requirements.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is FDI in India and how is it regulated?

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in India is regulated under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999 and the FDI policy issued by the DPIIT. FDI can be made through the automatic route or the government route, depending on the sector and the nature of the investment.

What is FC-GPR filing and when is it required?

FC-GPR is the form to be filed with the RBI within 30 days of allotment of shares to a foreign investor. It is required for all instances of FDI in Indian companies, including initial allotment, conversion of ECB into equity, and allotment under the scheme of arrangement.

What is the pricing guideline for FDI?

For unlisted companies, shares must be issued to non-residents at a price not less than the fair market value determined as per internationally accepted pricing methodologies, typically the DCF method. For listed companies, the pricing must comply with SEBI regulations.

What is downstream investment and when does it need to be reported?

Downstream investment refers to investment made by an Indian company with FDI in another Indian company. Such investments must be reported to the RBI and must comply with the same FDI conditions that apply to the parent entity.

What is the FLA return and when is it due?

The Annual Return on Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) must be filed by all Indian companies that have received FDI or made ODI. It is due by June 30 every year.
