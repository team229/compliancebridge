ODI Compliance

Overseas Direct Investment Compliance in India

Professional ODI compliance services for Indian entities investing abroad. APR filing, ODI reporting, overseas subsidiary compliance, and cross-border investment advisory.

Overview

Comprehensive ODI Compliance and Advisory Services

Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) is a critical component of India's foreign exchange regulatory framework. As Indian companies increasingly look to expand their global footprint through investments in foreign subsidiaries, joint ventures, and wholly-owned entities, compliance with ODI regulations has become essential. The ODI framework is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), the RBI Master Direction on Overseas Investment, and the Overseas Investment Rules and Regulations notified by the government.

ODI encompasses investments made by Indian entities in foreign entities through equity, compulsorily convertible debentures, or any other instrument prescribed under the regulations. This includes setting up of wholly-owned subsidiaries, joint ventures, acquisition of foreign companies, and other forms of overseas investment. Each type of ODI has specific regulatory requirements, reporting obligations, and compliance conditions that must be meticulously followed to avoid penalties and enforcement actions.

From pre-investment structuring and regulatory mapping to post-investment reporting and annual compliance, our team ensures that your overseas investment is fully compliant with all applicable regulations.

Key Facts

  • APR due date: December 31 annually
  • Investment limit: 400% of net worth
  • Governing law: FEMA and RBI Master Direction
  • Filing route: AD Category-I bank
  • Penalty: Up to 3x the amount involved
ODI Framework

ODI Regulatory Framework

Eligible Entities for ODI

Indian entities eligible to make ODI include companies under the Companies Act, LLPs under the LLP Act, body corporates under any law, and partnerships in certain cases.

Eligible Countries

ODI is permitted in all countries except those specifically restricted by the RBI or Ministry of Finance. Countries sharing a land border with India have specific restrictions.

Investment Limits

Under the automatic route, total financial commitment should not exceed 400% of net worth. For a single overseas entity, the limit is 400% of net worth or USD 1 billion, whichever is lower.

ODI Instruments

ODI can be made through equity shares, compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), compulsorily convertible preference shares (CCPS), and other prescribed instruments.

APR Filing

Annual Performance Report (APR) Filing

01

What is the APR?

The Annual Performance Report (APR) is an annual filing required from every Indian entity that has made overseas direct investment. It provides comprehensive information about the overseas entity including its financial position, performance, dividend details, and other prescribed information. It must be filed by December 31 every year and submitted through the AD Category-I bank on the RBI FIRMS portal.

02

Contents of the APR

The APR contains details of the Indian investing entity and the overseas entity, financial statements, dividend received or retained, capital infusion, disinvestment or restructuring details, loan and guarantee details, and business activities. The APR must be certified by the statutory auditor or a practicing chartered accountant.

03

Consequences of Non-Filing

Failure to file the APR attracts compounding proceedings before the RBI. Non-filing may impact the entity's ability to make further ODI, result in restrictions on repatriation of funds, and attract enhanced scrutiny. The AD bank may refuse to process further remittances until the APR is filed.

Reporting

ODI Reporting Requirements

ODI Form Filing

For each ODI transaction, the Indian entity must file the prescribed form through the AD bank, including initial ODI forms, capital infusion forms, and disinvestment or exit forms.

Modification Reporting

Any modification in the overseas entity structure including changes in shareholding pattern, name, business activities, or merger/demerger must be reported to the RBI within the prescribed timeline.

Dividend and Repatriation

Dividends received from overseas entities and other repatriation of funds must be properly reported. Retention of profits overseas beyond prescribed limits may require specific RBI approval.

Disinvestment and Exit

When an Indian entity disinvests or exits the overseas entity, it must file the prescribed form with the RBI including the board resolution, sale agreement, and proof of receipt of sale proceeds.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ODI under FEMA?

Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) refers to investment made by an Indian entity in a foreign entity, whether by way of equity, compulsorily convertible debentures, or any other instrument. ODI is governed by the RBI Master Direction on Overseas Investment and the FEMA regulations.

What is an APR in the context of ODI?

The Annual Performance Report (APR) is a mandatory annual filing that every Indian entity with overseas investment must submit to the RBI. It provides details of the overseas entity performance, financial position, and other prescribed information. APR must be filed by December 31 every year.

What are the eligible countries for ODI?

ODI is permitted in all countries except those specifically restricted by the RBI or Ministry of Finance. Countries sharing a land border with India have specific restrictions.

What is the limit for ODI by an Indian entity?

Under the automatic route, an Indian entity can make ODI up to 400% of its net worth as of the end of the previous financial year, subject to certain conditions.

When is prior RBI approval required for ODI?

Prior RBI approval is required for ODI in certain cases, including investment in restricted countries, where the Indian entity is under investigation, or has defaulted in filing returns.
