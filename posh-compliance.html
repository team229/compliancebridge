Overview

Expert POSH Compliance and Workplace Harassment Prevention

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013, commonly known as the POSH Act, is India landmark legislation mandating the prevention and redressal of sexual harassment at workplaces. Enacted following the Supreme Court guidelines in Vishakha vs. State of Rajasthan, the POSH Act places comprehensive obligations on every employer to create a safe and secure working environment for women. The Act applies to all workplaces including the organized and unorganized sectors, government and private organizations, and covers all women whether employed, interns, consultants, contractors, or visitors.

Non-compliance with the POSH Act carries severe consequences including monetary penalties up to Rs 50,000 for first-time violations, double penalties for subsequent non-compliance, and potential cancellation of business licenses and registrations. Beyond legal penalties, non-compliance exposes organizations to reputational damage, loss of employee trust, and potential litigation. In recent years enforcement of the POSH Act has become increasingly rigorous with district authorities conducting audits and issuing show-cause notices to non-compliant organizations.

POSH Act Framework

Understanding the POSH Act, 2013

The POSH Act provides a comprehensive framework for preventing and addressing sexual harassment at workplaces across India.

Definition of Sexual Harassment

Under Section 2(n) of the POSH Act sexual harassment includes any unwelcome acts or behavior such as physical contact and advances, demand or request for sexual favours, making sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography, and any other unwelcome physical verbal or non-verbal conduct of sexual nature. The Supreme Court in Vishakha vs. State of Rajasthan recognized two broader categories: quid pro quo harassment and hostile environment harassment.

  • Physical contact and advances
  • Demand for sexual favours
  • Sexually coloured remarks
  • Quid pro quo and hostile environment

Employer Obligations

The POSH Act places specific obligations on employers including constituting the ICC, developing and implementing a POSH policy, conducting regular awareness programs, displaying information about the POSH Act and ICC at the workplace, providing assistance to the complainant if needed, and filing the annual compliance report. Employers must also ensure that their contractors and service providers comply with POSH requirements.

  • Constitute ICC within prescribed time
  • Develop and implement POSH policy
  • Conduct annual awareness programs
  • File annual report with District Officer
ICC Constitution

Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) Constitution

Mandatory ICC constitution for every employer with 10 or more employees under Section 4 of the POSH Act.

01

ICC Composition Requirements

The ICC must consist of a presiding officer who is a senior woman employee of the organization, at least two members from among the employees who are committed to the cause of women or have experience in social work or legal knowledge, and one external member from a non-governmental organization or association committed to the cause of women. At least half of the ICC members must be women.

02

ICC Term and Reconstitution

The ICC members shall hold office for a period not exceeding three years from the date of their nomination. The ICC must be reconstituted upon expiry of the term and vacancies must be filled up within a prescribed period. If a member ceases to be an employee or commits any misconduct the vacancy must be filled up promptly.

03

ICC Inquiry Process

The ICC must complete the inquiry within 90 days from the date of receipt of the complaint. The inquiry must follow principles of natural justice and both the complainant and the respondent must be given a fair opportunity to present their case. The ICC may recommend interim relief if required and the findings must be based on the preponderance of probability standard.

Annual Reporting and Compliance

POSH Annual Report Filing and Compliance Management

Comprehensive annual compliance reporting and ongoing POSH compliance management for organizations.

Annual Report Filing

Under Section 21 of the POSH Act every employer must file an annual report with the District Officer containing the number of complaints received, complaints pending, complaints disposed of, and cases pending for more than 90 days. The annual report must be filed by January 31 of the following year for the preceding calendar year.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the POSH Act and who does it apply to?

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 applies to all workplaces in India including organized and unorganized sectors. Every employer with 10 or more employees must constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) and comply with the Act provisions.

What is an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)?

The ICC is a mandatory body constituted by every employer with 10 or more employees. It must include a presiding officer (senior woman employee), at least two employee members, and one external member from an NGO. The ICC handles complaints and must complete inquiries within 90 days.

What are the penalties for non-compliance with POSH Act?

Non-compliance can result in a fine of up to Rs 50,000 for first-time violations. For subsequent non-compliance the fine is doubled and the employer licence or registration may be cancelled. Failure to constitute ICC, file annual reports, or take action on complaints can all attract penalties.

What is the annual POSH compliance report?

Under Section 21 of the POSH Act every employer must file an annual report with the District Officer containing the number of complaints received, pending, disposed of, and cases pending for more than 90 days. The report must be filed by January 31 for the preceding calendar year.

Does POSH Act apply to virtual or remote workplaces?

Yes the POSH Act applies to virtual and remote workplaces. The definition of workplace includes any place visited by the employee arising out of or during the course of employment. Employers must extend POSH policies and complaint mechanisms to cover virtual workplaces and digital communication platforms.

What training is required under POSH compliance?

POSH compliance requires regular sensitization training for all employees, special training for ICC members, and awareness programs. The Act mandates employers carry out awareness programs through workshops and orientations. ICC members must receive training on conducting inquiries and following procedural requirements.
