Overview

Comprehensive NBFC Compliance and Regulatory Advisory

Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) play a vital role in India's financial ecosystem by providing credit and financial services to underserved sectors. Regulated by the Reserve Bank of India under the RBI Act, 1934, NBFCs must comply with a complex and evolving regulatory framework that governs their registration, operations, governance, capital adequacy, and reporting requirements.

The regulatory landscape for NBFCs has become significantly more stringent in recent years following the IL&FS crisis. The RBI has introduced enhanced governance norms, stricter capital requirements, tighter lending norms, and more rigorous reporting obligations. Non-compliance can result in cancellation of registration, monetary penalties, restrictions on business operations, and potential criminal proceedings against directors and officers.

  • Min NOF: Rs 2-5 crore

NBFC Categories

Types of Non-Banking Financial Companies

Based on Deposit Acceptance

NBFCs are primarily classified as NBFC-D (accepting public deposits) and NBFC-ND (not accepting public deposits). NBFC-D entities must maintain a minimum credit rating, deposit insurance, and comply with stringent deposit-related regulations. NBFC-ND entities have lighter compliance requirements but must still meet capital adequacy and governance norms.

Based on Activity Classification

NBFCs are further classified based on their principal activity: Investment and Credit Company (ICC), Micro Finance Institution (MFI), Infrastructure Finance Company (IFC), Infrastructure Debt Fund (IDF), Housing Finance Company (HFC), Asset Finance Company (AFC), Loan Company (LC), and Factor. Each category has specific regulatory requirements.

Systemically Important NBFCs

NBFC-ND-SI are non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size of Rs 500 crore and above. They are subject to enhanced regulatory requirements including higher capital adequacy ratios (12% CAR), ALM reporting, CRILC reporting, and more stringent governance norms comparable to banks.

Digital Lending and New Categories

The RBI has introduced new categories including Account Aggregators (NBFC-AA), NBFC Peer-to-Peer Lending Platforms, and digital lending guidelines applicable to all NBFCs. Digital lending NBFCs must comply with specific requirements including loan disbursal through bank accounts and use of RBI-regulated payment gateways.

Registration

NBFC Registration Process

01

Company Incorporation

The first step is to incorporate a company under the Companies Act, 2013 with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA). The company must have the object clause permitting it to carry on the business of an NBFC. The company should have a minimum of 2 directors and registered office in India.

02

Minimum Net Owned Fund (NOF)

The company must have a minimum net owned fund of Rs 2 crore for NBFC-ND and Rs 5 crore for NBFC-D. NOF is calculated as paid-up equity capital plus free reserves minus accumulated losses and intangible assets. The funds must be deposited in the company's bank account before applying for registration.

03

RBI Application (Form NBS-1)

An application in Form NBS-1 must be submitted to the Regional Office of the RBI. The application requires a detailed business plan, Fit and Proper declarations for all directors, KYC/AML policy, Board-approved policies, infrastructure details, and information about the management team. All documents must be certified by a Company Secretary in Practice.

04

RBI Processing and Approval

The RBI scrutinizes the application and may seek additional information or clarification. Once satisfied, the RBI issues a Certificate of Registration (CoR) with specific conditions. The company must commence business within 6 months of receiving the CoR. Failure to commence within the stipulated time results in lapse of the registration.

Compliance

Ongoing NBFC Compliance Requirements

Regulatory Reporting and Returns

NBFCs must file multiple regulatory returns with the RBI on quarterly and annual basis. These include NBS-1 (quarterly return on deposits for NBFC-D), NBS-7 (annual return on deposits), ALM statements (monthly/quarterly), CRILC returns (for borrowers above Rs 5 crore), XBRL returns for NBFC-ND-SI, and quarterly compliance certificates from Statutory Auditors.

Capital Adequacy Requirements

NBFCs must maintain a minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) computed as a percentage of risk-weighted assets. NBFC-D must maintain 15% CAR, NBFC-ND-SI must maintain 12% CAR, and other NBFCs must maintain 12% CAR. Capital consists of Tier I and Tier II capital with specific components allowed under each tier.

Governance and Board Requirements

NBFCs must comply with enhanced governance norms including appointment of minimum 3 directors with at least one independent director, constitution of Audit Committee, Nomination and Remuneration Committee, and Risk Management Committee. The Chairman and Managing Director must be approved by the RBI. Board-approved policies must be in place for all operational areas.

Fair Practices Code

NBFCs must adopt and implement a Board-approved Fair Practices Code covering loan application processing, credit appraisal, loan disbursement, interest rate policy, recovery practices, and grievance redressal mechanism. The Fair Practices Code must be displayed on the website and made available to borrowers. A Grievance Redressal Officer must be appointed.

Digital Lending

Digital Lending Compliance for NBFCs

01

Loan Disbursal Requirements

Under the RBI's digital lending guidelines, all loan disbursals must be made directly into the borrower's bank account through a regulated payment system. NBFCs cannot disburse loans through third-party lending service providers or their own virtual accounts. This ensures transparency and proper tracking of fund flows.

02

Lending Service Provider (LSP) Agreements

NBFCs engaging LSPs must enter into written agreements specifying the roles and responsibilities of each party. LSPs cannot collect repayments or hold customer funds. All customer data collected by LSPs must be stored by the NBFC with appropriate data security measures. The NBFC remains responsible for compliance regardless of LSP involvement.

03

Disclosure and Grievance Redressal

NBFCs engaged in digital lending must provide clear disclosure of APR, penal charges, cooling-off period, and recovery practices. A dedicated grievance redressal mechanism must be in place with the nodal Grievance Redressal Officer details prominently displayed. Complaints must be resolved within 30 days.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an NBFC?

A Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) is a company registered under the Companies Act, 2013, engaged in the business of loans and advances, acquisition of shares, debentures, securities, or other financial instruments. NBFCs do not include institutions whose principal business is agricultural activity, industrial activity, purchase or sale of any goods, or providing any services and sale/purchase/construction of immovable property.

What are the different types of NBFCs?

NBFCs are classified based on deposits (NBFC-D taking public deposits and NBFC-ND not accepting public deposits), based on activity (Investment and Credit Company, Micro Finance Institution, Infrastructure Finance Company, Infrastructure Debt Fund, Housing Finance Company, Factor, Asset Finance Company, Loan Company), and based on size (Systemically Important NBFCs or NBFC-ND-SI with asset size of Rs 500 crore and above).

How to register an NBFC with RBI?

NBFC registration requires: (1) Company incorporation under Companies Act 2013, (2) Minimum net owned fund of Rs 2 crore for NBFC-ND and Rs 5 crore for NBFC-D, (3) Application to RBI in prescribed format (Form NBS-1), (4) Submission of all required documents including business plan and Fit and Proper declarations, (5) RBI processing and in-principle approval, (6) Commencement of business within 6 months of approval.

What annual returns must NBFCs file with RBI?

NBFCs must file several returns including NBS-7 (annual return on deposits), NBS-1 (quarterly return on deposits), ALM statements, CRILC returns for borrowers above Rs 5 crore, XBRL returns for NBFC-ND-SI, filing of annual return with ROC, and quarterly compliance certificates as applicable.

What are the governance requirements for NBFCs?

NBFCs must comply with governance requirements including Board composition with minimum 3 directors and independent directors, appointment of Chairman/Managing Director with RBI approval, constituting Audit Committee, NRC, Risk Management Committee, adoption of Fair Practices Code, Grievance Redressal mechanism, Board-approved policies, capital adequacy norms (CAR of 15% for NBFC-D, 12% for NBFC-ND-SI), and CRILC reporting requirements.
