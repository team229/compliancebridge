Overview

Expert Board Advisory & Director Compliance

Effective board governance is the cornerstone of every successful company. The board of directors is responsible for the strategic direction, oversight, and accountability of the organization. Under the Companies Act 2013, directors are entrusted with fiduciary duties and are required to comply with a comprehensive set of statutory obligations spanning director appointment, DIN management, board meeting compliance, annual general meetings, and ongoing disclosure requirements. Non-compliance with these provisions can result in penalties, disqualification of directors, and adverse regulatory consequences.

Director Compliance

Director Compliance and DIN Management

We manage the complete lifecycle of director compliance, from DIN acquisition through appointment, ongoing obligations, and cessation processes.

DIN Acquisition and Management

A Director Identification Number (DIN) is mandatory for every person intending to be appointed as a director of a company under Section 153 of the Companies Act 2013. The DIN application is filed in Form DIR-3 with the MCA, and once allotted, it is valid for the lifetime of the director. We handle the complete DIN lifecycle including application filing, document verification, follow-up with the MCA, and annual DIN renewal through DIR-3 KYC filing by September 30 each year.

  • DIR-3 application filing and processing
  • Annual DIR-3 KYC renewal by September 30
  • DIN deactivation and reactivation support

Director Appointment and Resignation

Director appointment involves a structured process including board resolution, shareholder approval through ordinary resolution at general meeting, and ROC filing in Form DIR-12 within 130 days. Director resignation requires proper notice, board acceptance, and filing of DIR-12 with the ROC. We handle the complete process for both appointment and resignation of directors, ensuring compliance with Section 152, Section 169, and applicable rules. Our team also manages additional compliance requirements for independent directors, woman directors, and resident directors.

  • Board resolution and shareholder approval
  • DIR-12 filing with ROC within 130 days
  • Resignation acceptance and compliance

Board Meeting Compliance

Section 173 of the Companies Act 2013 mandates that every company hold at least four board meetings per year with a maximum gap of 120 days between consecutive meetings. Our board meeting support services cover the complete meeting cycle including drafting of notice and agenda, preparation of board papers, quorum verification, minutes drafting in compliance with Secretarial Standard SS-1, and post-meeting follow-up on action items. We also ensure compliance with the requirement of at least one board meeting per quarter for small companies and OPCs.

  • Minimum 4 meetings per year, 120-day gap
  • Notice, agenda, and board paper preparation
  • Minutes drafting per SS-1 compliance

Board Evaluation

Board evaluation is mandatory for listed companies and prescribed classes of public companies under Section 134(3)(p) of the Companies Act 2013 and Regulation 17(10) of SEBI LODR. The evaluation covers performance of the board as a whole, its committees, individual directors including the chairman. We design evaluation questionnaires, facilitate the evaluation process, compile results, and prepare action plans for improvement. Our methodology aligns with SEBI guidance on board evaluation and industry best practices.

  • Board, committee, and director evaluation
  • Customized evaluation questionnaires
  • Results compilation and action plans
Board Meetings & General Meetings

Board Meeting and AGM/EGM Compliance

We provide end-to-end support for board meetings, committee meetings, annual general meetings, and extraordinary general meetings with full statutory compliance.

Annual General Meeting (AGM)

Every company must hold an AGM within six months from the end of the financial year, with a maximum gap of 15 months between two consecutive AGMs. The AGM is the primary forum for shareholders to approve financial statements, declare dividends, appoint or ratify auditors, and transact other annual business. We handle the complete AGM process including notice preparation, proxy management, voter registration for e-voting, venue arrangements, and post-AGM filings with the ROC.

  • AGM within 6 months of financial year end
  • Notice, proxy, and e-voting management
  • Financial statements and dividend approval

Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM)

An EGM is convened to transact urgent business that cannot wait until the next AGM, such as special resolutions for alteration of articles, related party transactions, or other significant corporate actions. The board may convene an EGM on its own or upon requisition by shareholders holding at least one-tenth of the paid-up share capital. We manage the complete EGM process including convening resolution, notice preparation, special resolution compliance, and ROC filing of resolutions.

  • Board-convened and requisitioned EGMs
  • Special resolution compliance and filing
  • ROC filing of resolutions (MGT-14)
Independent Directors & Evaluation

Independent Directors and Board Evaluation

We provide specialized advisory on independent director compliance, appointment, term limits, and comprehensive board evaluation frameworks.

01

Independent Director Advisory

Independent directors play a critical role in corporate governance by providing objective oversight and ensuring that the board acts in the best interests of all stakeholders. Under Section 149 of the Companies Act 2013, listed companies must have at least one-third independent directors, and companies with an executive chairman must have at least half the board as independent directors. We advise on independence criteria under Schedule IV and SEBI LODR Regulation 16, assist in identifying suitable candidates, manage the appointment process including formal letter of appointment, and ensure compliance with term limits (two consecutive terms of five years each with a three-year cooling-off period).

02

Board Evaluation Framework

A robust board evaluation process is essential for continuous improvement in board effectiveness. We design and implement evaluation frameworks that assess the performance of the board as a whole, each board committee (audit, nomination and remuneration, stakeholder relationship), and individual directors including the chairman. Our evaluation methodology uses structured questionnaires covering areas such as board composition, quality of discussions, strategic oversight, risk management, and governance practices. We compile evaluation results, facilitate discussions on findings, and develop actionable improvement plans that are presented to the board.

03

Director Fiduciary Duties

Directors owe fiduciary duties to the company under Section 166 of the Companies Act 2013, including the duty to act in good faith, exercise due care and diligence, avoid conflicts of interest, and not make undue gains. We educate directors on their legal obligations and personal liability under the Companies Act, SEBI regulations, and other applicable laws. Our advisory helps directors understand the implications of related party transactions, interested director provisions, and disclosure requirements, enabling them to fulfill their responsibilities with confidence and compliance.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Director Identification Number (DIN) and who needs it?

A Director Identification Number (DIN) is a unique identification number assigned to every individual who is appointed as a director of a company under Section 153 of the Companies Act 2013. DIN is mandatory for anyone who wishes to serve as a director in any Indian company. The application is filed in Form DIR-3 with the MCA, and once allotted, the DIN is valid for the lifetime of the director. DIN must be renewed annually through DIR-3 KYC filing.

How many board meetings must a company hold in a year?

Under Section 173 of the Companies Act 2013, every company must hold a minimum of four board meetings in a calendar year, with a gap of not more than 120 days between two consecutive board meetings. The first board meeting of a newly incorporated company must be held within 30 days of incorporation. For small companies and OPCs, a minimum of two board meetings per half-year is sufficient, with a gap of at least 90 days between them.

What is the difference between AGM and EGM?

An Annual General Meeting (AGM) is a mandatory yearly meeting of shareholders that must be held within 6 months from the end of the financial year, with a gap of not more than 15 months between two AGMs. An Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) is called to transact urgent business that cannot wait until the next AGM. An EGM can be convened by the board on its own or on the requisition of shareholders holding at least 1/10th of the paid-up share capital. Both meetings require proper notice, agenda, and compliance with the Companies Act provisions.

What are the qualifications and disqualifications for directors?

Under Section 196 of the Companies Act 2013, a director must be a natural person who has attained the age of 18 years and holds a valid DIN. Disqualifications under Section 164 include: being of unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, having applied to be adjudicated as insolvent, convicted by a court of any offense involving moral turpitude or economic offense, and not having filed annual returns or financial statements for three consecutive years. For listed companies, additional independence criteria apply under SEBI LODR regulations.

What is the process for appointment of a director?

The appointment of a director follows a structured process: (1) Obtain DIN for the proposed director through Form DIR-3, (2) Board passes a resolution proposing the appointment, (3) Issue notice of general meeting with the proposed appointment as an agenda item, (4) Shareholders approve the appointment through ordinary resolution at the general meeting, (5) File Form DIR-12 with the ROC within 130 days of appointment. For casual vacancies, the board can appoint a director who holds office until the next general meeting.

What are the requirements for independent directors?

Under Section 149 of the Companies Act 2013, listed companies must have at least one-third of the total directors as independent directors. For companies with executive chairman, half the board must be independent. Schedule IV prescribes the code for independent directors, including requirements for independence, fiduciary duties, and obligations. Independent directors cannot hold office for more than two consecutive terms of five years each, though they can be reappointed after a cooling-off period of three years.
