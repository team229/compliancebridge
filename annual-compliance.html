Annual Compliance

Annual Compliance Services for Companies in India

Comprehensive annual compliance management for Indian companies. We ensure timely ROC filing, MCA compliance, and fulfillment of all statutory obligations to keep your company in good standing.

Overview

End-to-End Annual Compliance Management

Every company registered under the Companies Act 2013 is required to comply with a range of annual statutory obligations. These include filing annual returns and financial statements with the Registrar of Companies (ROC), holding board meetings and annual general meetings, maintaining statutory registers, filing director KYC, and adhering to various other compliance requirements. Failure to comply with these obligations attracts heavy penalties, director disqualification, and can even lead to the company being struck off from the ROC register.

We work with private limited companies, public limited companies, one-person companies, and LLPs, ensuring each entity meets its specific compliance obligations within the prescribed timelines.

Our approach is preventive rather than reactive. We proactively track all due dates, prepare compliance checklists, coordinate with your internal teams for required information, and ensure every filing is completed accurately and on time. This systematic approach not only avoids penalties but also builds a strong compliance culture within your organization, which is increasingly important for investor due diligence, banking relationships, and regulatory scrutiny.

Key Requirements

Annual Compliance Requirements

Companies must fulfill several annual compliance obligations to maintain good standing with the Registrar of Companies.

Annual Return (MGT-7 / MGT-7A)

Every company must file its annual return with the ROC within 60 days from the date of the Annual General Meeting. The annual return contains details of the company's registered office, principal business activities, particulars of holding and subsidiary companies, details of shares and debentures, members and debenture holders, and changes in directors and key managerial personnel during the financial year.

  • MGT-7: For companies with paid-up capital of ₹10 crore or more or turnover of ₹50 crore or more
  • MGT-7A: For OPC and small companies (simplified form)
  • Must be filed digitally signed by a director and certified by a Company Secretary

Financial Statements (AOC-4)

Every company must file its financial statements with the ROC within 30 days from the date of the AGM. The financial statements include the balance sheet, statement of profit and loss, cash flow statement, notes to accounts, and the auditor's report. The financial statements must be approved by the board of directors and adopted at the AGM.

  • AOC-4: For all companies other than OPC and small companies
  • AOC-4 CFS: For companies with subsidiaries (consolidated financial statements)
  • AOC-4 XBRL: For companies required to file in XBRL format

Director KYC (DIR-3 KYC)

Every individual who holds a DIN (Director Identification Number) as on March 31 of a financial year must file the DIR-3 KYC form with the MCA by September 30 of that financial year. This annual KYC requirement ensures that the MCA has up-to-date information about all directors.

  • Due date: September 30 every year
  • Penalty for late filing: ₹5,000
  • DIN gets deactivated if not filed

Board Meetings & AGM

Companies must hold a minimum of 4 board meetings every year with a gap of not more than 120 days between two consecutive meetings. The Annual General Meeting (AGM) must be held within 6 months from the end of the financial year. Proper notice, agenda, quorum, and minutes must be maintained for all meetings.

  • Minimum 4 board meetings per year
  • AGM within 6 months of financial year end
  • Proper notice and minutes documentation
  • Quorum requirements must be met

Non-Compliance Risks

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Non-compliance with annual filing requirements can have severe consequences for the company and its directors.

Heavy Penalties

Late filing attracts additional fees of ₹100 per day per form. For example, if AOC-4 and MGT-7 are both filed late, the combined penalty can be ₹200 per day. These penalties accumulate over time and can become substantial.

Director Disqualification

Under Section 164(2) of the Companies Act 2013, a director is disqualified if the company fails to file financial statements or annual returns for a continuous period of 3 financial years. This disqualification lasts for 5 years.

Company Strike-Off

If a company fails to file returns for a continuous period of 2 financial years, the ROC may initiate action to strike off the company from the register. This effectively dissolves the company and its assets may be treated as ownerless property.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the annual compliance requirements for a private limited company in India?

Annual compliance requirements include filing annual return (MGT-7), financial statements (AOC-4), director KYC (DIR-3 KYC), holding board meetings (minimum 4 per year), holding AGM, maintaining statutory registers, and filing event-based forms with the MCA as applicable.

What is the due date for filing annual return of a company?

The annual return (MGT-7/MGT-7A) must be filed within 60 days from the date of the Annual General Meeting (AGM). If the AGM is held on September 30, the due date for filing the annual return would be November 29.

What happens if a company fails to file annual returns on time?

Late filing attracts additional fees of ₹100 per day (for MGT-7) or ₹100 per day (for AOC-4) until the filing is completed. Continuous non-filing can lead to the company being struck off from the ROC register, and directors may face disqualification.

Is audit mandatory for all companies?

Yes, all companies registered under the Companies Act 2013 are required to get their financial statements audited by a practicing Chartered Accountant. The audit report is filed with the ROC along with the financial statements in Form AOC-4.

What is DIR-3 KYC and when is it due?

DIR-3 KYC is the annual KYC form that every director must file with the MCA to update their personal and contact details. It is due on September 30 every year. Late filing attracts a penalty of ₹5,000.
