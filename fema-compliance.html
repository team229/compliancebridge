Overview

Expert FEMA Compliance & Foreign Exchange Advisory

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA) is the cornerstone legislation governing all foreign exchange transactions in India. Enacted to consolidate and amend the law relating to foreign exchange with the objective of facilitating external trade and payments and promoting the orderly development and maintenance of the foreign exchange market in India, FEMA has become increasingly significant as India's economy integrates further with global markets. Every entity — whether a company, LLP, partnership, trust, or individual — that engages in cross-border transactions must comply with the provisions of FEMA and the various rules, regulations, and master directions issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

At ComplianceBridge Advisory, we bring deep expertise in FEMA compliance, helping businesses navigate the complex web of foreign exchange regulations. Our team of qualified Company Secretaries and regulatory professionals provides end-to-end compliance support covering foreign direct investment (FDI), overseas direct investment (ODI), external commercial borrowings (ECB), trade remittances, foreign currency accounts, and cross-border transactions. We work with startups, mid-size companies, large corporates, NBFCs, and foreign entities investing in India, ensuring full regulatory compliance while optimizing transaction structures.

Non-compliance with FEMA provisions can result in severe penalties, including monetary penalties up to three times the sum involved, adjudication proceedings, and potential criminal prosecution in cases of willful violations. The RBI has been increasingly active in enforcement actions, making proactive compliance a business imperative rather than a mere regulatory formality. Our preventive approach to FEMA compliance helps clients avoid these risks while maintaining the flexibility to execute cross-border transactions efficiently.

FDI Compliance

Foreign Direct Investment Compliance

FDI compliance involves adherence to FEMA regulations, RBI Master Directions, and press notes governing foreign investment in India.

FC-GPR Filing (Form Convertible Global Depository Receipts - Part A)

FC-GPR is the form to be filed with the RBI within 30 days of the allotment of shares to a foreign investor. This form covers initial allotment of shares to non-residents, including FDI, conversion of ECB into equity, conversion of foreign currency convertible bonds, and allotment of shares under the scheme of arrangement. The filing must be supported by necessary documents including board resolution, share allotment certificate, and valuation certificate.

  • Due within 30 days of share allotment
  • Filed through AD Category-I bank
  • Supported by valuation certificate

FC-TRS Filing (Transfer of Shares)

FC-TRS is filed when there is a transfer of shares between a resident and a non-resident, or vice versa. This applies to transfers of shares in an Indian company from a non-resident to a resident (inbound transfer) or from a resident to a non-resident (outbound transfer). The form must be filed within 60 days from the date of transfer of shares. The transfer must comply with the pricing guidelines prescribed under the FDI policy.

  • Due within 60 days of share transfer
  • Pricing guidelines compliance required
  • Covers resident-to-non-resident and vice versa

FDI Reporting Requirements

Indian companies receiving FDI must comply with various reporting requirements including submission of the Annual Return on Foreign Liabilities and Assets (FLA) by June 30 every year. Companies must also report the details of downstream investment in Indian companies to the RBI. Active compliance with sectoral caps, pricing guidelines, and conditionalities is essential for maintaining a clean compliance record.

  • FLA return due by June 30 annually
  • Downstream investment reporting
  • Sectoral cap compliance monitoring

FDI Pricing Guidelines

The pricing of shares issued to non-residents and transferred between residents and non-residents must comply with RBI pricing guidelines. For unlisted companies, shares must be issued/transferred at a price not less than the fair market value determined as per internationally accepted pricing methodologies. For listed companies, the SEBI regulations govern the pricing. Proper valuation by a registered valuer is essential.

  • DCF method for unlisted companies
  • SEBI guidelines for listed companies
  • Registered valuer certification required
ODI & ECB Compliance

Overseas Investment & External Borrowings Compliance

FEMA compliance extends to overseas investments and external commercial borrowings, each governed by specific RBI regulations and reporting requirements.

Overseas Direct Investment (ODI)

ODI compliance involves reporting and regulatory adherence for Indian entities making investments abroad. This includes filing of the Annual Performance Report (APR) for each overseas entity, reporting of modification in the structure of overseas entities, and compliance with the RBI Master Direction on Overseas Investment. ODI regulations prescribe eligible countries, eligible entities, investment limits, and reporting timelines.

  • APR filing within prescribed timelines
  • Reporting of disinvestment and structure changes
  • Compliance with eligible country criteria
  • ODI application for new investments

External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)

ECB compliance covers the regulatory framework governing borrowing by Indian entities from foreign lenders. This includes ECB-2 filing with the RBI, compliance with all-in-cost ceiling, end-use restrictions, minimum average maturity period, and reporting requirements. The ECB framework under the RBI's Master Direction on ECB and Overseas Borrowings prescribes detailed conditions for eligible borrowers, eligible lenders, and permissible end-uses.

  • ECB-2 form filing within 30 days
  • All-in-cost ceiling compliance
  • End-use and maturity period monitoring
  • Hedging requirements tracking

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is FEMA and who does it apply to?

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA) is the primary legislation governing foreign exchange transactions in India. It applies to all persons resident in India and those dealing in foreign exchange, including companies, LLPs, partnerships, and individuals involved in cross-border transactions.

What is the penalty for FEMA violations?

Under Section 13 of FEMA, penalties for contravention can extend up to three times the sum involved in the contravention, or up to ₹2 lakh where the sum is not quantifiable. For continuing contraventions, an additional penalty of ₹5,000 per day may be imposed.

What is the difference between current account and capital account transactions under FEMA?

Current account transactions relate to ordinary business operations such as trade in goods and services. Capital account transactions involve changes in assets or liabilities, such as foreign direct investment, external commercial borrowings, and portfolio investment. Both have different regulatory requirements.

Do I need RBI approval for all foreign exchange transactions?

Not all transactions require RBI approval. Many transactions under the automatic route can be completed without prior approval, though post-transaction reporting may be required. Transactions under the government route or those not covered by general permissions require prior approval.

What is FEMA compounding?

FEMA compounding is a mechanism under Section 15 of FEMA that allows voluntary compounding of contraventions. It enables applicants to compound an alleged contravention by paying a compounding amount as determined by the Compounding Authority, without going through the full adjudication process.

How often do FEMA regulations change?

FEMA regulations and RBI Master Directions are updated periodically, sometimes multiple times in a year. The RBI regularly amends foreign exchange norms through master directions, circulars, and notifications. It is essential for businesses to stay updated on these changes.
