External Commercial Borrowing Compliance in India

Professional ECB compliance services for Indian entities availing foreign currency borrowings. ECB-2 filing, reporting, eligible borrower analysis, and end-use compliance.

Overview

Comprehensive ECB Compliance and Advisory Services

External Commercial Borrowing (ECB) is a significant source of financing for Indian entities seeking foreign currency funds from overseas lenders. The ECB framework allows eligible Indian borrowers to access international capital markets and foreign funding sources at competitive rates. ECB is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), the RBI Master Direction on External Commercial Borrowings, and the ECB framework issued by the RBI.

The ECB framework encompasses various instruments including foreign currency-denominated loans, buyer's credit, supplier's credit, securitized instruments like fixed-rate bonds and floating-rate notes, and foreign currency bonds. Each type of ECB has specific eligibility criteria for borrowers and lenders, end-use restrictions, all-in-cost ceiling requirements, minimum average maturity periods, and reporting obligations that must be carefully adhered to.

Key Facts

  • ECB-2 due date: Within 30 days of month-end
  • Min. maturity: 3 years (average)
  • Governing law: FEMA and RBI Master Direction
  • Filing route: AD Category-I bank
  • Penalty: Compounding proceedings before RBI
ECB Framework

ECB Regulatory Framework

Eligible Borrowers

Eligible borrowers include all entities that are eligible to avail foreign exchange loans, including companies, LLPs, registered trusts and societies, SEZ units, and entities in certain infrastructure sectors as specified by the RBI.

Eligible Lenders

Recognized lenders include international banks, financial institutions, foreign equity holders (for specific ECB categories), foreign export credit agencies, and other lenders as specified by the RBI from time to time.

End-Use Restrictions

ECB proceeds cannot be used for certain restricted activities including investment in real estate, investment in stock market, on-lending, or any purpose not approved by the RBI. End-use compliance is a critical aspect of ECB regulations.

All-In-Cost Ceilings

The all-in-cost ceiling for ECB is determined based on the benchmark rate plus a spread. For loans with a minimum average maturity of 3 years, the all-in-cost is benchmarked accordingly, with different ceilings for different tenors.

ECB-2 Filing

ECB-2 Filing and Reporting

01

Monthly ECB-2 Return

ECB-2 is a monthly return that must be filed by every entity that has drawn down ECB. It provides details of the ECB drawn during the month, outstanding amount, repayment, and other prescribed information. The return must be filed within 30 days from the end of the month through the AD bank.

02

ECB Utilization Certificate

After full drawdown of the ECB, the borrower must submit the ECB utilization certificate to the RBI through the AD bank, certifying that the ECB has been utilized for the stated end-use purpose. This must be submitted within a prescribed timeframe from the last drawdown.

03

Penalty for Non-Compliance

Failure to file the ECB-2 return within the prescribed timeline attracts compounding proceedings before the RBI. The penalty can be up to 2% per annum of the amount of default, compounded for the period of default. Non-compliance may also impact the entity's ability to avail future ECB.

ECB Structures

Types of ECB Structures

Foreign Currency Loans

The most common form of ECB is a loan availed from a recognized foreign lender in foreign currency. These loans can be with fixed or floating interest rates, with varying tenors and repayment structures.

Buyer's and Supplier's Credit

Buyer's credit is a financing arrangement where a foreign lender provides funds directly to the Indian importer. Supplier's credit is extended by the foreign supplier to finance the purchase of goods or services.

Securitized Instruments

ECB through securitized instruments includes fixed-rate bonds, floating-rate notes, and other capital market instruments. These are governed by specific conditions including minimum average maturity and end-use requirements.

ECB from Foreign Equity Holders

Indian companies can avail ECB from their foreign equity holders under specific categories. This includes ECB for investments in infrastructure, general corporate purposes, and other permitted end-uses as per the ECB framework.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is External Commercial Borrowing (ECB)?

ECB is a loan availed by an eligible Indian entity from a recognized non-resident lender, outside India. It includes commercial bank loans, buyers credit, suppliers credit, securitized instruments, and foreign currency bonds.

Who are eligible borrowers for ECB?

Eligible borrowers include all entities that are eligible to avail foreign exchange loans, including companies, LLPs, registered trusts and societies, SEZ units, and certain other entities as specified by the RBI.

What is ECB-2 filing?

ECB-2 is the monthly return that must be filed with the RBI through the AD bank providing details of ECB drawn, outstanding, and repayment. The return is filed within 30 days from the end of the month.

What are the all-in-cost ceiling rates for ECB?

The all-in-cost ceiling varies based on the tenor and benchmark rate. For loans with a minimum average maturity of 3 years, the benchmark rate plus 500 basis points is the typical ceiling.

Can ECB be used for working capital purposes?

Yes, ECB can be used for working capital purposes provided the entity meets the eligibility criteria, the minimum average maturity requirement is satisfied, and the ECB is within the prescribed all-in-cost ceiling.
